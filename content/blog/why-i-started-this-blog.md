When I stepped into college to study Computer Science and Engineering at Integral University, Lucknow, I didn't arrive with a grand master plan. Like most first-year students, I brought curiosity, an interest in technology, and an eagerness to understand how code transforms into software that people actually use.

Over the first two and a half years of my B.Tech, that curiosity led me down several paths. I experimented with different programming languages, built web interfaces, participated in hackathons, and gradually realized that my strongest technical interests were coalescing around **Data Science** and **Artificial Intelligence**.

Along the way, I noticed a recurring problem in how students approach tech education: **we often consume massive amounts of information without taking the time to synthesize what we actually understand.**

This blog—*Learning in Progress*—is my attempt to change that habit.

---

## Moving Beyond Generic Tutorials

The internet is saturated with cookie-cutter tutorials. You can find thousands of identical explanations of a Python `for` loop, basic SQL syntax, or a copy-pasted machine learning script. What is far less common is an honest, ongoing account of the actual learning curve:

- The conceptual roadblocks that take days of head-scratching to unravel.
- The mistakes made when architecting early semester projects or hackathon prototypes.
- The friction of balancing regular university coursework with independent technical exploration and exam preparation.
- The realization that running a pre-built library is completely different from understanding the underlying mathematics.

I wanted an independent website that felt less like a promotional portfolio and more like a laboratory journal.

The turning point for me arrived as I entered my third year. Between building our hackathon prototype ResourceBridge, navigating core computer science coursework at Integral University, and committing to prepare systematically for GATE DA 2027, I realized that passive study was no longer enough. I needed a structured system of personal accountability. Writing publicly forces me to break complex ideas down into simple terms, verify derivations, and honestly assess what I have truly mastered versus what I have merely skimmed.

---

## What This Publication Will Document

As I progress through the remainder of my undergraduate degree, this journal will focus on seven key areas:

1. **My Engineering Journey:** Honest reflections on studying Computer Science at Integral University, balancing lab work, exams, and personal growth.
2. **Programming:** Developing clean code habits, mastering Python, and understanding data structures and algorithms from the ground up.
3. **Data Science:** Exploring datasets, statistics, exploratory data analysis (EDA), data cleaning, and finding empirical signal in raw numbers.
4. **Artificial Intelligence:** Investigating theoretical foundations, search algorithms, knowledge representation, and agent systems.
5. **Machine Learning:** Breaking down supervised and unsupervised models, loss functions, optimization, and evaluation metrics without hiding behind black-box libraries.
6. **Projects and Experiments:** Transparent breakdowns of things I build (including hackathon prototypes like ResourceBridge)—what worked, what broke, and what I would refactor.
7. **Career and Learning:** Documenting my multi-year preparation for the **GATE Data Science and AI (DA) 2027** examination, along with practical study techniques.

## An Open Invitation to Learn in Public

Writing about your learning in public is inherently vulnerable. It means acknowledging what you do not know yet and accepting that early code or explanations might need correction.

However, learning in public creates discipline. When you know an article will be read by peers, seniors, and mentors, you are forced to test your assumptions thoroughly before making claims.

If you are a fellow engineering student or someone exploring data and computing from first principles, I hope these notes offer a grounded, authentic perspective.
