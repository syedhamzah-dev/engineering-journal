When I stepped into engineering, I didn't arrive with an encyclopedic understanding of what computer science truly entailed. Like most students entering college, I brought curiosity, a broad fascination with computers, and an eagerness to see how real-world software actually came together.

Over the first two and a half years of my B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering, that curiosity pushed me in many directions. I experimented with different programming languages, wrestled with foundational algorithms, explored data manipulation, and eventually found myself captivated by the mathematics and possibilities of Data Science and Artificial Intelligence.

Yet, amidst all the assignments, exams, and rapid tutorials, I noticed an recurring problem: **we often consume information without deeply synthesizing it.**

This blog—*Learning in Progress*—is my response to that problem.

---

## Moving Beyond Generic Tutorials

The internet is filled with superficial tech news and cookie-cutter tutorial clones. You can find ten thousand identical explanations of a `for` loop or a basic linear regression script. What is far rarer is an honest, ongoing log of the actual learning process:

- The conceptual roadblocks that take days to unravel.
- The architectural mistakes made in early semester projects.
- The shift from merely copying boilerplate code to understanding the underlying mathematical mechanics.
- The balance between university coursework, independent technical deep-dives, and competitive exam preparation (such as GATE Data Science and AI 2027).

I wanted an independent space that felt less like a promotional portfolio and more like a rigorous laboratory notebook.

## What This Publication Will Cover

As I continue through my third and fourth years, this journal will center around seven distinct threads:

1. **My Engineering Journey:** Raw, candid reflections on balancing academics, lab work, setbacks, and personal growth.
2. **Programming:** Building solid foundations in Python, understanding clean code, data structures, and software design principles.
3. **Data Science:** Moving through exploratory data analysis, statistics, pandas, data wrangling, and discovering stories within messy data.
4. **Artificial Intelligence:** Investigating theoretical foundations, heuristic search, agent architecture, and knowledge representation.
5. **Machine Learning:** Breaking down supervised and unsupervised models, cost functions, optimization, and real-world evaluation metrics.
6. **Projects and Experiments:** Detailed post-mortems of things I build—what worked, what broke, and what I would do differently.
7. **Career and Learning:** Documenting my multi-year roadmap towards becoming an AI Engineer / Data Scientist, including structured preparation for the GATE DA 2027 examination.

---

## An Open Invitation to Learn in Public

Swallowing your ego and documenting mistakes publicly is uncomfortable. But learning in public creates accountability. It forces you to verify your assumptions before putting them into writing, and it opens up dialogues with peers, seniors, and mentors who can offer constructive critique.

If you are a fellow student navigating engineering, an aspiring data professional, or someone passionate about technology from first principles, I hope these notes resonate with your own journey.

*The journey is only just beginning.*
